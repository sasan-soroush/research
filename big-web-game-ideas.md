# Big Web Game Ideas: Research Report

*Researched October 2026. Goal: a web-first game with the potential to earn **millions of dollars per year in profit**, grow steadily and last 5–10+ years.*

---

## 1. TL;DR

| Rank | Concept | Revenue ceiling | Longevity | Build difficulty | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Persistent social voxel world + seasonal clan wars** ("Minecraft × Rust × Roblox-lite, in a browser tab") | $10–100M+/yr | Very high | High | **Best overall bet** |
| **2** | **Browser-native strategy MMO with seasonal servers** (modern Travian / Forge of Empires) | $10–50M/yr | Very high (these games run 15–20+ yrs) | Medium–High | **Safest proven money** |
| **3** | **Web-native competitive shooter / hero arena** (Krunker evolved, built for Discord + portals + esports) | $5–50M/yr | High if the competitive scene sticks | High | Strong, but more hit-driven |
| 4 | AI-native social deduction / party platform (Discord Activities first) | $2–20M/yr | Medium–High | Medium | Fastest to viral reach |
| 5 | Cozy social farming / life-sim MMO (FarmVille successor, done right) | $5–50M/yr | High | Medium–High | Huge, under-served audience |
| 6 | Creature-collection RPG with web shop (cross-platform, D2C) | $10–100M/yr | High | Very high (art and content) | Big ceiling, content-heavy |
| 7 | Daily-puzzle network on subscription (NYT Games model) | $1–10M/yr | Very high | Low–Medium | Profitable, smaller ceiling |

**My recommendation:** build **#1** if you can fund a team of 8–20 for 18–24 months. Build **#2** if you want the most predictable path to eight-figure revenue. Either way, **launch everywhere the web runs**: CrazyGames, Poki, Discord Activities, YouTube Playables and your own domain. Then wrap the same build for iOS and Android with a **web shop**, so most purchases skip the 30% app-store cut.

## 2. Why the web, and why now (2026)

1. **Distribution exists at scale.**
   - CrazyGames reported **50M monthly active users** in 2025 and launched **in-app purchases for developers**.
   - Discord says Apps and Activities are used by **over 25% of its MAU**, with reported figures of **80M+ monthly users playing an Activity** (unverified, secondary source).
   - Telegram mini-apps reach a claimed 1B+ MAU, and YouTube Playables is growing.
2. **The tech ceiling is gone.** Safari 26 shipped **WebGPU on by default**, so WebGPU now runs in every major browser. Console-quality rendering in a tab is becoming realistic, though WebGL2 is still the safe baseline.
3. **App-store economics broke open.**
   - After the April 2025 US Epic v. Apple injunction, developers can link US iOS players to web stores with no Apple commission. Industry estimates put that at roughly **$0.92 kept per $1 on the web versus about $0.70 in-store**.
   - Over 60 of the top-100 grossing mobile games now run a web shop, and direct-to-consumer (DTC) game payments grew about 46% year on year in 2025.
   - Epic Web Shops (launched October 2025) charge 0% on the first $1M.
   - A web-first game is therefore *already* set up for the highest-margin business model in games.
4. **Zero-install = lowest acquisition cost in gaming.** A link in Discord, TikTok or a YouTube description becomes a player in about 5 seconds. Every player can recruit the next one with one click, and **no other platform matches that viral loop**.
5. **Proof that it scales.**
   - **Bloxd.io** (voxel sandbox) reported about 580K daily players and a 20K concurrent peak in July 2025, and is described as self-funded and profitable. It was CrazyGames' #1 game in June 2026.
   - **Krunker** sustained a studio for years on cosmetics and tournaments, and Krunker Strike more than doubled its players after launching on Discord.
   - **Death by AI** hit about 700K DAU and roughly 7M players within weeks on Discord.
   - **Slither.io** became a multi-million-dollar property.
   - **Shell Shockers** has run since 2017.
6. **The gap:** about 15,000 new web games launched in **one quarter** of 2025, and almost all are small hyper-casual or ad-funded games. **Almost nobody is building a *big*, deep, live-service game for the web.** That gap is the opportunity.

**The honest caveat:** web ad revenue per player is low (cents per daily user). Big money on the web comes from **IAP, battle passes, subscriptions and the web shop**, not ads. Ads are a bonus for non-payers.

## 3. The longevity checklist (what every idea is scored against)

Games that grow for a decade, such as RuneScape, Travian, Minecraft, Roblox, League and Fortnite, share these traits:

| # | Box | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Social obligation**: clans, guilds, friends who expect you | The #1 retention driver. People quit games; they don't quit their friends. |
| 2 | **Player-driven content**: PvP, economy, politics or UGC | Players make content faster than you can. No content treadmill. |
| 3 | **Seasons / resets** | A fresh start every 2–3 months brings back lapsed players and gives new ones a fair entry point. |
| 4 | **Long-term progression and collection** | Account-level growth that survives season resets (cosmetics, titles, collections). |
| 5 | **Mastery and competition**: ranked play, leaderboards, esports | Gives core players years of goals and creates streamable moments. |
| 6 | **Viral by design**: shareable links, spectate-in-browser, invite rewards | Free growth. On the web, every share is one click from a new player. |
| 7 | **Fair, high-ceiling monetization**: cosmetics, battle pass, subscription, convenience | Whales spend on status, everyone else on the pass. No pay-to-win anger. |
| 8 | **Low friction, deep game**: playable in 10 seconds, still interesting after 1,000 hours | Web-specific. Instant start is the web's superpower, and depth keeps people. |
| 9 | **Cross-platform**: the same account on web, mobile, desktop and Discord | Players never "leave" your game, they just switch screens. |
| 10 | **Creator / streamer friendly** | TikTok, YouTube and Twitch are the new marketing. Chaos, drama and clips are what spread. |

## 4. The ideas in depth

### #1: Persistent Social Voxel World + Seasonal Clan Wars ⭐ *Top pick*

**Pitch:** A huge, persistent, destructible voxel world in the browser. Players gather, build bases and towns, form clans, trade, and fight over territory. Every **season (about 10 weeks)** the map resets with a new biome, new mechanics and a new story event. Your account, cosmetics and blueprints carry over. Between seasons, **creative servers and minigames** (built by you first, later by players) keep everyone playing.

Think **Minecraft's building × Rust's clan drama × Roblox's minigame variety**, with zero install.

**Why it can be huge**
- It sits in the proven sweet spot: Minecraft, Roblox, Bloxd.io and Fortnite Creative show the audience is massive and young.
- **Bloxd.io proves the web version works**, and it's still a fairly small team's game. A polished, deeper, better-monetized version is a clear opening.
- Clan wars create **drama**, and drama is free YouTube and TikTok content ("we raided the biggest clan's castle").
- Seasonal resets give you **a re-launch every 10 weeks**: a marketing beat, a returning-player spike and a new battle pass.
- Long-term it becomes a **platform**. Add a creator tool with revenue sharing, and players build the minigames for you. That's how Roblox became a $1B+ creator economy.

**Monetization**
- Season battle pass ($5–10)
- Cosmetic skins, emotes, building block palettes and base themes
- Premium subscription ($5–8/month): extra build plots, private servers, cosmetic perks, priority queue
- Private and rented servers for friend groups, schools and streamers (recurring)
- Later: a UGC marketplace where you take a cut of creator sales
- **No pay-to-win.** Never sell power in PvP.

**Checklist score: 10/10.** Ticks every box.

**Risks**
- Server cost for persistent physics and voxel worlds. Mitigate with sharded regions and authoritative-but-lightweight servers.
- Cheating in PvP. You need server authority from day one.
- Moderation, because the audience is young. Budget for chat filtering and reporting from the start.

**How to start small:** ship **one season-based survival-PvP server mode** plus **one creative mode** on CrazyGames and Poki first. Prove that 30-day retention and clan formation work, then expand.

### #2: Browser Strategy MMO with Seasonal Servers 🏰 *Safest proven money*

**Pitch:** A modern take on Travian, Tribal Wars, Forge of Empires and Rise of Kingdoms, built for the browser first. Thousands of players share one map. You grow a city, join an alliance, and spend weeks in diplomacy, betrayal and war to conquer world wonders. Servers run for **2–4 months**, then a new world opens with a twist (new civilization, new map, new rules).

**Why it can be huge**
- **This genre is the oldest proven money-maker on the web.** Travian (2004) and Tribal Wars (2003) still run 20+ years later. Forge of Empires (InnoGames, browser-first) became one of the highest-grossing strategy games and later moved to mobile.
- On mobile, 4X strategy games (Rise of Kingdoms, Whiteout Survival, Last War) are among the **highest-grossing games in the world**, with very high revenue per player.
- Alliance politics is the strongest social glue in gaming. People log in at 3 AM because their alliance needs them.
- It runs on cheap tech: tick-based and asynchronous, with no real-time physics. **Server costs are tiny, so margins are huge.**
- The browser versions of this genre are old and dated. **A modern, beautiful, fair version, with Discord integration so alliances can plan inside Discord, would stand out.**

**Monetization**
- Gold and premium currency for build-queue slots and cosmetic city skins
- VIP subscription
- Season pass
- Alliance-wide cosmetic purchases ("gift to your alliance" is an incredibly strong whale mechanic)
- Keep it **"pay-for-convenience, not pay-to-win"** as a brand differentiator. Mobile 4X is notorious for whales dominating, and fairness is your marketing.

**Checklist score: 9/10.** It is weaker on instant-fun and streamability, so you need great UX and clip-worthy war moments (battle replays you can share as a link).

**Risks:** onboarding is slow (strategy games take time to "click"). Long-term success depends on balance and anti-multi-accounting.

---

### #3: Web-Native Competitive Shooter / Hero Arena 🎯

**Pitch:** A fast, skill-based shooter or hero brawler (think Overwatch or Valorant-lite, or a stylized Counter-Strike) that loads in seconds, runs at high FPS on a school Chromebook, and is **built from day one for Discord Activities, portals and esports**.

**Why it can be huge**
- Shooters are the most-played genre on web portals (Krunker, Shell Shockers, Venge.io, Kirka.io). Demand is proven.
- Krunker Strike more than doubled its players by launching as a Discord Activity, so **social platforms amplify shooters**.
- Ranked play, clans and tournaments give core players years of play.
- Skins are the most proven high-margin item in games (Counter-Strike's skin economy is a multi-billion-dollar phenomenon).

**Monetization:** a cosmetic economy (skins, knives, charms, finishers), battle pass, ranked seasons, and sponsored tournaments and creator codes.

**Checklist score: 8/10.** Strong on mastery, competition and streamability. Weaker on deep social obligation unless you build clans and team ladders properly.

**Risks:** very crowded, and hit-driven ("game feel" has to be perfect). Cheating is a constant war. Needs regional servers worldwide.

---

### #4: AI-Native Social Party Platform 🎭

**Pitch:** A collection of party and social-deduction games (Among Us, Jackbox, Werewolf-style) where **an AI game master** generates scenarios, plays characters, judges answers and narrates. It launches inside **Discord Activities first**, then on the web and on TV.

**Why:** **Death by AI** reached about 7M players within weeks on Discord, and **Chef Showdown** has 14M+ players. Party games spread by themselves, since each session pulls in 4–10 friends. AI makes content effectively infinite.

**Monetization:** premium "party pass" (one buyer unlocks for the whole group), themed content packs, and cosmetic avatars.

**Score: 7/10.** Massive reach, but party games historically **spike and fade**. To get longevity you must turn it into a *platform* with new games every month, persistent profiles and competitive seasons. LLM inference costs also eat into margin, so design around cheap models and caching.

**Use it as:** a **fast first project** to build an audience, cash flow and Discord expertise before (or alongside) #1 or #2.

---

### #5: Cozy Social Farming / Life-Sim MMO 🌾

**Pitch:** Stardew Valley and Animal Crossing as a shared online world. You get your own farm and home, a town shared with friends, a player-run market, seasonal festivals, and cooperative town projects.

**Why:** FarmVille proved the web audience (tens of millions of daily players on Facebook in 2010). Cozy games are among the fastest-growing segments, with a huge female and adult audience who **pay reliably** and stay for years. Competition on the web is almost nonexistent at a high quality level.

**Monetization:** cosmetics (decoration is the core loop, so people buy a *lot* of it), seasonal passes, land expansions and subscription.

**Score: 8.5/10.** Very strong retention and monetization. Less "viral chaos" for TikTok, but strong on Instagram and Pinterest-style sharing ("look at my farm").

---

### #6: Creature-Collection RPG with a Web Shop 🐉

**Pitch:** Pokémon-style collecting, breeding, trading and battling in an online world, with PvP ladders and seasonal regions.

**Why:** It has one of the largest proven audiences in gaming, and **Palworld** showed the appetite for alternatives. Collection is the most powerful long-term retention hook there is.

**Score: 8/10**, but **very content-heavy**: hundreds of creatures need art, animation and balance. This is the most expensive idea to do well. Avoid anything that looks like an IP clone, because the legal risk is real.

---

### #7: Daily Puzzle Network on Subscription 🧩

**Pitch:** A daily-habit games bundle (word, logic, number and geography games) with streaks, friend leagues and a subscription.

**Why:** NYT bought Wordle for about $1M, and it brought "tens of millions" of new users. NYT Games sells a standalone sub (about $1.50/week). Daily games are among the most loyal habits on the internet.

**Score: 7/10.** Very low cost, very high margin and very long life, but a ceiling of a few million per year unless you get exceptional hits. **A great side business, not the "think big" play.**

## 5. Revenue math: what "millions per year" actually needs

The web's revenue per daily user is lower than mobile's, so plan with conservative numbers:

| Scenario | DAU | Avg revenue / DAU / day (IAP + ads) | Annual revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid hit | 100K | $0.05 | ~$1.8M |
| Strong | 300K | $0.08 | ~$8.8M |
| Big | 1M | $0.10 | ~$36M |
| Breakout (mobile wrap + web shop) | 2M | $0.15 | ~$110M |

*These are planning assumptions, not market data. Strategy and MMO games typically earn a higher daily revenue per player; io games earn lower.*

**Profit drivers:**
- Web-shop margins of about 92% versus about 70% in app stores.
- Low server costs, especially for strategy games.
- Organic virality, which cuts paid acquisition.

A strategy MMO or social world with **300K DAU and 3–5% paying users can plausibly clear $5M+ in yearly profit**.

## 6. The go-to-market playbook (applies to any pick)

1. **Build web-first and mobile-friendly from day one.** About 64% of browser-game players are reportedly on phones and tablets. Touch controls are not optional.
2. **Launch on the portals:** CrazyGames (50M MAU, now with IAP), Poki and others. These are free traffic and a fast feedback loop. Use them to tune retention before spending on marketing.
3. **Ship a Discord Activity version.** Social games played *with* friends retain far better, and Discord takes 15% on the first $1M. Add YouTube Playables and Telegram for extra reach.
4. **Own your domain and accounts.** Portals are channels, not your home. Push players to create an account on *your* site so you own the relationship, the payments and the data.
5. **Wrap for iOS and Android** once retention is proven. Use a **web shop** for purchases (legal for US iOS link-outs since 2025; Google lowered fees after the March 2026 Epic settlement, per a single source).
6. **Creator program from launch:** creator codes with revenue share, streamer-only private servers, and clip and replay sharing built into the game.
7. **Live ops cadence:** a new season every 8–12 weeks, a weekly event, and a public roadmap. **Consistency is longevity.**
8. **Metrics to hit before scaling spend:** D1 ≥ 35%, D7 ≥ 15%, D30 ≥ 7% (web benchmarks are lower than mobile; beat them and you have a hit).

---

## 7. Reality check

- **Team and budget.** A competitive version of #1, #2 or #3 is realistically **6–20 people over 12–24 months** (or a small team plus heavy AI tooling for code and art). Solo, start with the narrowest playable slice (one mode, one season) and grow it in public.
- **Tech stack suggestions:**
  - Three.js or Babylon.js (WebGL2 with optional WebGPU), or PlayCanvas, for 3D.
  - Phaser or PixiJS for 2D strategy and farming.
  - Colyseus, Nakama or a custom Go/Rust server for multiplayer.
  - Cloudflare or Fly.io for global edge regions.
- **Avoid:** pay-to-win, IP clones, crypto/NFT economies (they consistently hurt trust and longevity), and launching without anti-cheat and moderation.
- **Data quality note:** market-size estimates for web games vary wildly ($3B–$23B) depending on definitions, and many per-game figures above come from secondary sources. Treat the numbers as directional.

---

## 8. Final recommendation

> **Build #1, the Persistent Social Voxel World with Seasonal Clan Wars, as the flagship.** It ticks every longevity box, rides the biggest audience in gaming (young sandbox players), and has a proven web precedent (Bloxd.io) that leaves lots of room above it. Long-term it can grow into a UGC platform.
>
> **If you want lower risk:** build #2 (Strategy MMO). It's the most proven web money-maker in history, cheap to run, and the browser competition is dated.
>
> **If you need early cash and audience:** ship #4 (AI party game on Discord) as a 3–4 month side project while the flagship is built.
